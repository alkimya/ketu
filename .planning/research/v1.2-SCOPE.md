## Tier 1 — Structural features

These are the milestone's headline deliverables. Each rests on the houses primitive shipped in v1.1.

### House systems extension (registry already in place)

- **Whole Sign** — every house = one zodiac sign starting at the sign of the Ascendant. Trivial trig, polar-safe. Hellenistic revival favorite.
- **Equal** — house cusps spaced 30° from the Ascendant. Trivial trig, polar-safe.
- **Regiomontanus** — celestial equator division projected through the prime vertical. More complex trig; popular in horary astrology.

**Why now:** The `SYSTEMS` registry pattern was specifically designed for this in Phase 10. These are the natural next concrete implementations to validate the extensibility claim. Whole Sign and Equal are also polar-safe (unlike Placidus/Koch), which strengthens the polar story.

**Backlog reference:** `HOU2-01`, `HOU2-02`, `HOU2-04` from `.planning/milestones/v1.1-REQUIREMENTS.md`.

### Synastry (chart comparison)

Inter-chart aspect calculation: take two natal charts (e.g. two people), compute aspects between body N of chart A and body M of chart B. The dominant relational analysis tool. Foundation for compatibility readings.

**Required primitives:**
- A "Chart" abstraction (positions + ASC/MC + houses + aspects packaged together) — Ketu doesn't have this yet; each calculation is independent
- Vectorized cross-product of aspects between two body sets
- Output dtype that preserves which chart each body came from

**Open question for research:** is this a new `SYNASTRY_DTYPE` extending `ASPECT_DTYPE`, or a parameterization of existing `calculate_aspects`?

### Composite chart

Mathematical fusion of two charts into a single "relationship chart" via midpoints — for each body, take the midpoint of the two natal longitudes (modulo 360°). The composite is then read as a normal chart.

**Required primitives:**
- Midpoint helper on circular longitudes (handles 359° + 1° → 0°, not 180°)
- Reuses synastry's "Chart" abstraction
- Houses are computed from midpoint MC + composite ASC

**Synergy:** synastry and composite share the "two charts in, derived data out" workflow. Treat them as one design effort, not two.

### Solar return (révolution solaire)

Compute the chart for the exact moment in a target year when the Sun returns to its natal longitude (typically near the user's birthday, but not exactly — can be hours or a day off). Used for yearly forecasting.

**Required primitives:**
- Root-finding on `Sun_longitude(t) - natal_Sun_longitude` over a ~24h window around the anniversary
- Newton-Raphson or scipy-style brentq — but pure-NumPy contract means we either ship our own bisection/secant or vectorize the Newton step
- Chart computation at the resolved instant (reuses Tier 1 chart abstraction)
- Optional: relocate to a different lat/lon (relocated solar return)

**Subtle point:** the user's birth lat/lon is needed for the *natal* Sun longitude, but the *return* chart can be cast for any location (relocation). Need to expose both knobs cleanly.

## Tier 2 — Useful primitives

Lower-cost additions that round out the toolkit.

### Part of Fortune + Arabic Parts framework

**Part of Fortune (Lot of Fortune):** the canonical part. Day formula `ASC + Moon − Sun`, night formula `ASC + Sun − Moon` (sect-dependent). Symbol ⊗. Almost universally displayed in modern charts.

**Framework:** an extensible `PARTS` registry analogous to the `SYSTEMS` houses registry. Each part is a tuple `(name, day_formula, night_formula)` where formulas are functions of `(asc_lon, body_lons)`. Ship 5-10 essential parts:

- Part of Fortune (mandatory)
- Part of Spirit (Hellenistic complement to Fortune)
- Part of Eros, Part of Necessity, Part of Courage, Part of Victory, Part of Nemesis (the seven Hermetic Lots)
- Part of Marriage (relational, complements synastry/composite work)

**Cost:** low. Pure arithmetic on longitudes, no new astronomical code, no new dependencies. Vectorizable trivially. Maybe 1-2 plans.

**Sect determination:** "day" if Sun is above the horizon (above ASC-DESC line) at the chart's moment, "night" otherwise. Need a `is_day_chart(jd, lat, lon) -> bool` helper that's vectorizable.

## Cross-cutting design challenge: the `Chart` abstraction

Three Tier 1 features (synastry, composite, solar return) all need the same primitive that does **not** exist in v1.1:

> Given `(jd, lat, lon, house_system)`, return a fully-resolved chart — body positions + ASC/MC + houses + aspects — as a single structured object.

Today Ketu computes each of those independently. v1.2 will likely introduce either:

- **Option A:** a `CHART_DTYPE` structured array (or nested dtype) returned by a new `compute_chart(jd, lat, lon, system)` API
- **Option B:** a lightweight `Chart` dataclass holding the constituent NumPy arrays
- **Option C:** keep things flat — accept that synastry/composite/solar-return each take 4-6 parameters and don't introduce a new abstraction

Option A maximizes ML-interop (NumPy-first contract, structured arrays are first-class in v1.0). Option B is more Pythonic but less batchable. Option C keeps the API surface flat but pushes the burden onto callers.

**This is a real architectural decision and deserves a dedicated discussion phase early in v1.2.** Don't let it leak across 4 phase plans implicitly.

## Out of scope (deferred to v1.3)

- **Chiron** — Centaur asteroid, very widely used in modern astrology (more than Lilith) but **requires either swisseph as a dependency, an embedded ephemeris file (~50-200 KB Chebyshev coefficients), or a custom polynomial fit pipeline**. The user wants to explore the polynomial-fit option (Chebyshev-by-segment is performant — likely faster than current Mars/Jupiter — but requires ~1-2h spike + serious validation effort). Decision: defer to a dedicated v1.3 milestone where Chiron is the headline (likely with Centaurs more broadly: Pholus, Nessus, etc.).
- **True / Osculating Lilith (h13)** — instantaneous apogee variant. Rare in practice; Mean Apogee covers ~95% of usage. Defer.
- **Asteroid Lilith #1181** — different body entirely from h12 Mean Lilith. Niche. Defer.
- **Asteroids (Ceres, Pallas, Juno, Vesta), fixed stars** — same dependency-or-fit problem as Chiron. Defer to post-Chiron milestone (v1.4+).
- **Transits / progressions / directions** — predictive techniques beyond solar return. Could be v1.3 alongside Chiron, or a v1.4 of its own. The line between "solar return" (in v1.2) and "transits" (deferred) is: solar return is one chart at one resolved time; transits are continuous time-series over a window. Different problem shape.

## Risk register (preview)

To be expanded during research phase. Initial flags:

1. **Chart abstraction architecture decision** is upstream of three features. Get it wrong, all three pay. Plan a dedicated discussion phase.
2. **Solar-return root-finding** in pure NumPy needs care — Newton-Raphson on a wrap-around longitude function has subtle edge cases (e.g., Sun longitude jumps 360°→0° once per year). Either pre-shift or use unwrapped degrees throughout the search.
3. **Synastry aspect output volume** scales as O(N×M) where N, M are body counts. With 12 bodies × 12 bodies × N aspects, output sizes grow fast. Need to think about whether to return a dense matrix, a filtered list, or both modes.
4. **Sect determination for Parts** requires `is_day_chart()` which depends on ASC. ASC depends on house system. Make sure the dependency chain is explicit.
5. **Whole Sign / Equal "houses"** are arguably not house *calculations* — they're trivial functions of the Ascendant sign or longitude. The registry abstraction may need a "trivial" tier that bypasses the iteration machinery. Check for over-engineering.
6. **Backward compat:** v1.2 should be additive — no BREAKING changes after the v1.1 burn. Frame it explicitly as a non-breaking minor release.
